Family law in Newnan, Georgia

Divorce and custody are decided on the details. We help you get those details right.

Two things decide most family cases

Paperwork and timing. Not speeches. The parent whose records are organized, whose calendar is documented and whose filings are on time tends to do better, regardless of who is more sympathetic.

We start by figuring out what you actually want the end to look like, then work backward to the filings that get there.

Divorce

Georgia allows both no fault and fault based grounds. Most of our cases resolve by agreement, which keeps the terms in your hands instead of a judge's. The pieces that have to be settled are the same either way: how property and debt are divided, whether either spouse needs support, and what happens with the children.

Custody and parenting plans

Georgia courts decide custody on the best interests of the child, and they want a written parenting plan. A good plan is boring and specific. It says who has the children on a Tuesday in October, who drives, what happens when a flight is delayed and how the holiday schedule works in a leap year.

  • Legal custody, which is decision making, and physical custody, which is time
  • Week on week off, 2 2 3, and schedules built around shift work
  • Right of first refusal, travel notice and communication rules
  • Relocation, which almost always means a modification

Support and modifications

Child support in Georgia runs off a worksheet, with both incomes, health insurance and childcare in the calculation. Deviations are possible but have to be justified in writing. When circumstances change substantially, support and custody can both be revisited.

A parenting plan written for the family you were is the one that ends up back in court. We write for the family you are now.
