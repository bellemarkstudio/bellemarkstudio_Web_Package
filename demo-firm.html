The firm

Fifteen years in the same courthouse.

Brightwater Legal Group is a small Newnan firm built around one idea: the people who call a lawyer are having a bad week, and the least we can do is pick up.

Why the firm exists

Rachel Hollis spent four years prosecuting cases in the Coweta Judicial Circuit. What she noticed was not that defendants were poorly represented in court. It was that they could not reach their lawyer. Calls went to voicemail on a Friday and were returned on a Tuesday, and in between, people made decisions alone that they could not take back.

She opened Brightwater in 2011 with one rule: someone answers. Fifteen years later the firm is three attorneys and three staff, and that rule is still the thing clients mention most.

How we work

We keep caseloads small on purpose. An attorney who is carrying two hundred files cannot know yours, and you can tell within one phone call. We would rather turn a case away than take one we cannot give attention to.

We are also direct about money. Fees are flat where we can make them flat, hourly where the work genuinely cannot be predicted, and contingency on injury cases. You get the number and the structure in writing before anything starts.

If you leave our office less worried than you walked in, and you understood everything we said, we did the first part of the job right.

Where we practice

Most of our work is in the State and Superior Courts of Coweta County, with regular appearances in Fayette, Carroll, Heard and Meriwether. We handle appeals to the Georgia Court of Appeals and take federal matters in the Northern District when a case calls for it.

About the assistant that answers

Calls and website chats outside office hours are answered by an automated assistant. It says so, clearly, every time. It does not give legal advice and it never tells anyone whether they have a case. What it does is take your name, your number and a short reason, flag anything urgent, and get it in front of an attorney, usually within a couple of minutes.

It exists because the alternative is voicemail, and voicemail at 11 p.m. on a Saturday is how people end up talking to the police without counsel.

Reach us any hour

Our assistant answers nights and weekends, takes your details and flags anything urgent for an attorney.
