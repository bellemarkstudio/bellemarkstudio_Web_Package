DUI defense

Georgia’s 30 day license rule, explained plainly

Your license is handled in a separate process from your criminal case, and it moves first. Here is how that timeline actually works.

October 2, 2026DUI defenseAbout 4 min

Two cases, two clocks

When you are arrested for DUI in Georgia, two separate things start. One is the criminal charge, which goes to court and takes months. The other is an administrative action against your driver's license, run by the Department of Driver Services. They are not the same case, they do not share a calendar, and winning one does not automatically win the other.

The license side is faster. That is what catches people.

What the notice actually is

If you refused testing, or tested over the limit, the officer generally takes your license and hands you a form that doubles as a temporary permit. That form is not a receipt. It is notice that a suspension is coming and that you have a limited window to do something about it.

The window

You have a short, fixed number of days from the date of arrest to respond. In practice there are two routes, and which one fits depends on your record and the facts of the stop:

  • Request a hearing. This puts the suspension on hold and gets the officer in a room where their account can be tested under oath. It also costs a filing fee.
  • Install an ignition interlock device. This route trades the hearing for a permit that keeps you driving under conditions, and it is not available to everyone.

Doing neither is itself a choice, and it is the one that ends with a suspension taking effect automatically.

The criminal case is about whether you are convicted. The license case is about whether you can get to work next month. People focus on the first and lose the second.

Why a hearing helps even when you lose it

An administrative hearing puts the arresting officer on the record early, months before trial, answering questions about the stop, the testing and the procedure. That transcript does not disappear. If the account changes later, the earlier version is sitting there.

Attorneys who handle DUI regularly will often request the hearing for that reason alone.

If the window already closed

It is not necessarily over, but the options narrow. There may be reinstatement routes, a limited permit, or relief tied to the outcome of the criminal case. Those take longer and give you less control. Call anyway and find out what is left.
