Criminal defense

What a first appearance actually looks like

It is short, it is confusing, and it is over before most families understand what happened. Here is the whole thing in order.

August 26, 2026Criminal defenseAbout 4 min

What it is

A first appearance is a short hearing, usually within 48 hours of an arrest, where a magistrate tells the person what they are charged with, advises them of their rights and decides whether to set a bond. It is not a trial. Nobody is deciding guilt. Families show up expecting a day in court and get something that takes four minutes.

In order, what happens

  1. The charges are read. This is the first time many people hear the exact charge rather than what the officer said at the scene.
  2. Rights are explained, including the right to an attorney and the right to stay silent. This is said to everyone at once in some courts.
  3. Bond is addressed. The magistrate either sets an amount, denies bond, or says the charge is one only a superior court judge can bond.
  4. Conditions may be attached. No contact orders, travel limits, ankle monitoring, surrender of a passport.
  5. The next date is given. Usually an arraignment weeks out.
The single best thing a family can do before first appearance is have an attorney who already knows the file. Four minutes is enough time to make an argument, but only if somebody prepared one.

What the magistrate is weighing

Bond decisions turn on two questions: is this person likely to come back to court, and are they a danger to anyone in the meantime. Ties to the area, steady work, family nearby, no history of missing court. Those are the facts worth putting in front of the court, and they land better when a lawyer presents them than when a relative shouts them from a bench.

What not to do

  • Do not let the person explain their side at first appearance. It is not the forum, and it is recorded.
  • Do not assume a high bond is final. Bond can be revisited, and in superior court matters it often is.
  • Do not discuss the case on a jail phone line. Those calls are recorded and routinely used.

If you are watching from the gallery

Write down the charge exactly as it is read, the bond amount, any conditions and the next court date. That one note saves an attorney an hour of chasing paperwork and lets them start on the actual problem.
