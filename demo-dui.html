DUI defense in Newnan, Georgia

An arrest is not a conviction. The first ten days decide what happens to your license.

A DUI charge is really two cases

Most people arrested for DUI in Georgia think they have one problem: a court date. There are two. One is the criminal charge, which moves slowly and can take months. The other is your driver's license, which is handled by the Department of Driver Services on a far shorter clock.

That second case is the one people lose by accident. If nothing is filed in time, the license suspension takes effect on its own while the criminal case is still months away from being resolved.

The question in the first week is almost never "am I guilty." It is "what do we have to file right now so you can still drive to work."

What we look at first

Before anything else, we go through the stop itself. An officer needs a lawful reason to pull a car over and a separate reason to start a DUI investigation. If either one is thin, everything after it gets weaker.

  • The reason for the stop. Weaving inside your own lane, a tag light out, an anonymous call. Each of those has been argued both ways in Georgia courts.
  • Field sobriety testing. These are standardized tests with written rules. Officers do not always follow them, and the video usually shows it.
  • The breath machine. Calibration records, the twenty minute observation period, and whether the implied consent notice was read correctly and at the right time.
  • Your statements. What was asked, when, and whether you had been told you were free to leave.

First offense does not mean minor

A first DUI in Georgia still carries jail exposure, a fine, community service, a clinical evaluation and probation, plus insurance consequences that outlast all of it. For drivers under 21 and for anyone with a commercial license, the thresholds and the penalties are different and stricter.

What we need from you

Bring whatever you have. The citation, the bond paperwork, the implied consent form if you were given one, and the date on any notice from the Department of Driver Services. If you do not have any of it, call anyway. We can usually find most of it.
