DUI defense

The first 48 hours after a DUI arrest in Georgia

What you do the day after an arrest matters more than most people realize, starting with a deadline almost nobody is told about.

October 14, 2026DUI defenseAbout 4 min

Hour one: say less than you want to

The urge after an arrest is to explain. You want the officer, the magistrate, anyone, to understand that this is not who you are. That instinct is reasonable and it is also the single most common way people make their case harder.

You have to give your name and your license. You do not have to narrate your evening. "I would like to speak with an attorney" is a complete sentence, and repeating it is not rude.

Hours one to twelve: the paperwork you leave with

When you are released, you will have papers. People shove them in a glovebox and find them a month later. Photograph all of them that night. The ones that matter most are the citation with your court date, the bond paperwork, and anything titled a notice of suspension.

That last one is the one with the clock on it.

Day one: the deadline nobody mentions

Georgia handles your license in a separate administrative process from your criminal charge, and it moves first. There is a short window after the arrest to challenge the suspension, and it is counted in calendar days, not business days. Miss it and the suspension takes effect on its own, while the criminal case is still months from being heard.

Almost every license people lose unnecessarily is lost in the first two weeks, by doing nothing.

Day one: write it down while it is fresh

Before the details blur, write out the evening in order. What you ate and when. What you drank and when. Who was with you. What the officer said and in what order. Which tests you were asked to do and on what surface. Whether you were told you were free to leave.

In two months you will not remember whether the roadside was sloped or whether the officer asked about the smell before or after you got out of the car. Those details decide motions.

Day two: call a lawyer, not your group chat

Everybody has a cousin with a DUI story. The stories are usually wrong, because the law changed, or the county was different, or the person never actually knew what happened in their own case.

An initial call is free at most firms, including ours. Twenty minutes with someone who does this weekly is worth more than a week of searching.

What not to do

  • Do not post about it. Screenshots show up in court more often than people expect.
  • Do not call the arresting officer to explain.
  • Do not pay the citation online to make it go away. On most DUI citations that is not even an option, and where it is, it is a guilty plea.
  • Do not assume a breath result ends the case. Machines have maintenance records, and the procedure around the test has rules.
